# Normal Distribution in Machine Learning

The Normal (or Gaussian) Distribution is one of the most important probability distributions in statistics and machine learning, serving as the underlying assumption for many algorithms and statistical tests.

## 1. Core Concepts & Properties
A continuous probability distribution that is symmetrical around its center, forming a characteristic bell-shaped curve. 

* **Parameters:** It is defined entirely by two parameters: the mean ($\mu$) which determines the center, and the standard deviation ($\sigma$) which determines the spread.
* **Notation:** $X \sim N(\mu, \sigma)$.
* **Symmetry & Central Tendency:** The left and right sides of the distribution are mirror images. Because of this perfect symmetry, the Mean, Median, and Mode are all exactly equal.
* **Probability Density Function (PDF):** The mathematical equation governing the curve is:
    $$y = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\right)^2}$$
* **Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF):** Calculates the probability that a value $X$ is less than or equal to $x$ by integrating the PDF from $-\infty$ to $x$.

## 2. The Standard Normal Variate (Z)
Standardizing a normal distribution allows you to compare completely different datasets on the same scale and calculate probabilities universally.

* **Definition:** A specific normal distribution where the mean is **0** and the standard deviation is **1** ($Z \sim N(0, 1)$).
* **Z-Score Formula:** To convert any raw data point $x$ into a standardized Z-score, use:
    $$Z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$$
* **Usage:** Once standardized, you can use a Z-table (or a programmatic equivalent) to find the exact area under the curve (probability) to the left of the Z-score.

## 3. The Empirical Rule (68-95-99.7)
This rule provides a quick heuristic for understanding data dispersion in a normally distributed dataset.

* **$\sim$ 68.27%** of the data falls within **1** standard deviation of the mean ($\mu \pm 1\sigma$).
* **$\sim$ 95.45%** of the data falls within **2** standard deviations of the mean ($\mu \pm 2\sigma$).
* **$\sim$ 99.73%** of the data falls within **3** standard deviations of the mean ($\mu \pm 3\sigma$).

## 4. Skewness
Skewness measures the asymmetry of a distribution, indicating how far it deviates from a perfect normal distribution. 

* **Zero Skew:** A perfectly symmetrical normal distribution has a skewness of **0**.
* **Positive (Right) Skew:** The tail extends further to the right. The Mean is pulled toward the tail, making **Mode < Median < Mean**.
* **Negative (Left) Skew:** The tail extends further to the left. The Mean is pulled left, making **Mean < Median < Mode**.

## 5. Applications in Data Science & Machine Learning
Understanding if your data follows a normal distribution dictates which algorithms and transformations you can use.

* **Algorithm Assumptions:** Models like Linear Regression (which assumes normally distributed residuals) and Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM) rely heavily on this distribution.
* **Outlier Detection:** Using the Empirical Rule, any data point beyond $\mu \pm 3\sigma$ (in the 0.27% tail) can mathematically be flagged as an anomaly or outlier.
* **Hypothesis Testing:** Forms the basis for parametric tests like T-tests and Z-tests.
* **Central Limit Theorem (CLT):** States that the sampling distribution of the sample mean approaches a normal distribution as the sample size gets larger, regardless of the original data's distribution.
