# Lasso Regression (L1)

## 1. The Core Difference: Lasso vs. Ridge
* **Ridge (L2):** Adds the squared magnitude of weights ($\lambda \sum W_i^2$). It shrinks coefficients close to zero but rarely exactly zero.
* **Lasso (L1):** Stands for *Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator*. It adds the absolute magnitude of weights ($\lambda \sum |W_i|$). It shrinks the coefficients of less important features to **exactly zero**.

**Loss Function Formula:**
$$L = \text{MSE} + \lambda \sum |W_i|$$

## 2. The Biggest Advantage: Feature Selection
In datasets with high dimensionality (e.g., adding many polynomial degrees), you often end up with a lot of "noise" or useless columns. Because Lasso pushes the coefficients of these useless features to exactly zero, it effectively drops them from the equation entirely. This performs automatic **feature selection**, leaving you with a simpler, faster, and more interpretable model.

## 3. Interview Question: Which to prefer, Ridge or Lasso?
* **Choose Lasso:** If you suspect your dataset has a lot of useless features and you want a simpler model that only keeps the most important variables.
* **Choose Ridge:** If you believe most features are useful and contribute to the output, and you just want to prevent them from becoming too dominant.
* **Pro-Tip:** If you are unsure, mention **Elastic Net** in the interview. It combines both L1 and L2 penalties to give you the benefits of both.

## 4. Impact of Lambda ($\lambda$) on Bias & Variance
* **Overfitting State:** The model has low bias but **very high variance** (it fluctuates wildly on new data).
* **Low $\lambda$ (Close to 0):** The model acts like standard regression, maintaining that high variance risk.
* **High $\lambda$:** The penalty becomes strict. The model sacrifices a little accuracy on the training data (increasing **bias**) to become much more stable on unseen data (decreasing **variance**).

## 5. Why Does Lasso Hit Exactly Zero? (The Geometric View)
* **Ridge (L2)** creates a **circular** constraint region. The error ellipse usually touches the circle smoothly somewhere off the axes, so coefficients stay above zero.
* **Lasso (L1)** creates a **diamond-shaped** constraint region (a square rotated 45 degrees). The diamond has sharp corners perfectly aligned with the axes. When the error ellipse expands to touch the constraint, it almost always hits one of these sharp non-differentiable corners, forcing that coefficient to snap to exactly zero.
