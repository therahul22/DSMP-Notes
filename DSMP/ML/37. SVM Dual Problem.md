# Support Vector Machines (SVM) — Lecture 4: Dual Formulation & KKT Conditions

## 1. $N$-Dimensional Setup & Primal Optimization

In an $n$-dimensional feature space with input features $\mathbf{x} = (x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n)^T$ and target output $y_i \in \{+1, -1\}$, the separating hyperplane is given by:

$$
\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x} + b = 0 \quad \iff \quad w_1 x_1 + w_2 x_2 + \dots + w_n x_n + b = 0
$$

### Primal Formulations

* **Hard Margin SVM Primal:**

$$
\min_{\mathbf{w}, b} \frac{1}{2} \|\mathbf{w}\|^2 \quad \text{subject to } y_i(\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_i + b) \ge 1, \quad \forall i=1, \dots, n
$$

* **Soft Margin SVM Primal:**

$$
\min_{\mathbf{w}, b, \boldsymbol{\xi}} \left( \frac{1}{2} \|\mathbf{w}\|^2 + C \sum_{i=1}^{n} \xi_i \right) \quad \text{subject to } y_i(\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_i + b) \ge 1 - \xi_i, \quad \xi_i \ge 0
$$

> **Note:** We use $\frac{1}{2} \|\mathbf{w}\|^2$ instead of $\|\mathbf{w}\|$ for mathematical convenience, as its derivative simplifies cleanly to $\mathbf{w}$.
>

## 2. Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) Conditions

The **KKT conditions** generalize Lagrange multipliers to handle **inequality constraints**. For a general problem:

$$
\min f(\mathbf{x}) \quad \text{subject to } g_i(\mathbf{x}) \le 0
$$

The Lagrangian is $L(\mathbf{x}, \boldsymbol{\lambda}) = f(\mathbf{x}) + \sum \lambda_i g_i(\mathbf{x})$. The 4 KKT conditions are:

1. **Stationarity:** $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} L = 0$
2. **Primal Feasibility:** $g_i(\mathbf{x}) \le 0, \quad \forall i$
3. **Dual Feasibility:** $\lambda_i \ge 0, \quad \forall i$
4. **Complementary Slackness:** $\lambda_i \cdot g_i(\mathbf{x}) = 0, \quad \forall i$

---

### Walkthrough 1: Single Variable KKT Example

$$
\min_{x} f(x) = x^2 \quad \text{subject to } x - 1 \le 0
$$

1. **Lagrangian:** $L(x, \lambda) = x^2 + \lambda(x - 1)$
2. **Stationarity:** $\frac{\partial L}{\partial x} = 2x + \lambda = 0 \implies x = -\frac{\lambda}{2}$
3. **Primal Feasibility:** $x - 1 \le 0$
4. **Dual Feasibility:** $\lambda \ge 0$
5. **Complementary Slackness:** $\lambda(x - 1) = 0$

* **Case A ($\lambda = 0$):** Stationarity gives $x = 0$. Checks feasibility: $0 - 1 \le 0$ (Valid!). Minimum at $x = 0, f(0) = 0$.

---

### Walkthrough 2: Two Variable KKT Example

$$
\min_{x,y} f(x,y) = x^2 + y^2 \quad \text{subject to } x + y - 1 \le 0
$$

   Lagrangian: L(x, y, λ) = x² + y² + λ(x + y - 1)

1. **Stationarity Equations:**

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial x} = 2x + \lambda = 0 \implies x = -\frac{\lambda}{2}
$$

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial y} = 2y + \lambda = 0 \implies y = -\frac{\lambda}{2}
$$

2. **Complementary Slackness:** $\lambda(x + y - 1) = 0$

* **Testing Active Constraint ($\lambda > 0 \implies x + y - 1 = 0$):**

$$
\left(-\frac{\lambda}{2}\right) + \left(-\frac{\lambda}{2}\right) - 1 = 0 \implies -\lambda = 1 \implies \lambda = -1
$$

  *(Fails Dual Feasibility: $\lambda \ge 0$ is violated!)*

* **Testing Inactive Constraint ($\lambda = 0$):**

$$
x = 0, \quad y = 0
$$

  Check Primal Feasibility: $0 + 0 - 1 = -1 \le 0$ (Valid!).

**Optimal Solution:** $(x^*, y^*) = (0, 0)$ with $f(0,0) = 0$.

---

## 3. Step-by-Step Derivation of the SVM Dual Problem

### Step 1: Form the Lagrangian
Convert inequality constraints $y_i(\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_i + b) \ge 1 \implies 1 - y_i(\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_i + b) \le 0$ into unconstrained form using Lagrange multipliers $\alpha_i \ge 0$:

$$
L(\mathbf{w}, b, \boldsymbol{\alpha}) = \frac{1}{2} \|\mathbf{w}\|^2 - \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i \big[ y_i(\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_i + b) - 1 \big]
$$

Expanding the summation:

$$
L(\mathbf{w}, b, \boldsymbol{\alpha}) = \frac{1}{2} \|\mathbf{w}\|^2 - \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i y_i \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_i - b \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i y_i + \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i
$$

---

### Step 2: Apply Stationarity Conditions

Set partial derivatives with respect to primal variables ($\mathbf{w}$ and $b$) to zero:

1. **Derivative w.r.t $\mathbf{w}$:**

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial \mathbf{w}} = \mathbf{w} - \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i y_i \mathbf{x}_i = 0 \implies \mathbf{w} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i y_i \mathbf{x}_i \quad \text{--- (Equation 1)}
$$

2. **Derivative w.r.t $b$:**

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial b} = -\sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i y_i = 0 \implies \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i y_i = 0 \quad \text{--- (Equation 2)}
$$

---

### Step 3: Substitute Back to Form the Dual Objective

Substitute Equation 1 ($\mathbf{w} = \sum \alpha_i y_i \mathbf{x}_i$) and Equation 2 ($\sum \alpha_i y_i = 0$) back into $L(\mathbf{w}, b, \boldsymbol{\alpha})$:

1. The $b$ term vanishes because $\sum \alpha_i y_i = 0$.
2. The $\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|^2$ term becomes:

$$
\frac{1}{2} \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{w} = \frac{1}{2} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i y_i \mathbf{x}_i \right)^T \left( \sum_{j=1}^{n} \alpha_j y_j \mathbf{x}_j \right) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} \alpha_i \alpha_j y_i y_j (\mathbf{x}_i \cdot \mathbf{x}_j)
$$

3. The cross term becomes:

$$
-\sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i y_i \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_i = -\sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} \alpha_i \alpha_j y_i y_j (\mathbf{x}_i \cdot \mathbf{x}_j)
$$

Combining both terms ($\frac{1}{2} - 1 = -\frac{1}{2}$):

$$
W(\boldsymbol{\alpha}) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i - \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} \alpha_i \alpha_j y_i y_j (\mathbf{x}_i \cdot \mathbf{x}_j)
$$

---

### Step 4: Final Dual Formulations

#### Hard Margin SVM Dual Form:

$$
\max_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i - \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} \alpha_i \alpha_j y_i y_j (\mathbf{x}_i \cdot \mathbf{x}_j) \right)
$$

$$
\text{subject to } \alpha_i \ge 0, \quad \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i y_i = 0
$$

#### Soft Margin SVM Dual Form:

$$
\max_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i - \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} \alpha_i \alpha_j y_i y_j (\mathbf{x}_i \cdot \mathbf{x}_j) \right)
$$

$$
\text{subject to } 0 \le \alpha_i \le C, \quad \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i y_i = 0
$$

---

## 4. Why is the Dual Form Easier & More Powerful to Solve?

### 1. Sparsity & Support Vectors
By KKT Complementary Slackness:

$$
\alpha_i \big[ y_i(\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_i + b) - 1 \big] = 0
$$

* **Non-Support Vectors:** $y_i(\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_i + b) > 1 \implies \mathbf{\alpha_i = 0}$.
* **Support Vectors:** $y_i(\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_i + b) = 1 \implies \mathbf{\alpha_i > 0}$.

Since $\mathbf{w} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i y_i \mathbf{x}_i$, **all non-support vectors drop out completely** ($\alpha_i = 0$). The decision boundary depends strictly on the small subset of support vectors!

### 2. Enables the Kernel Trick
The dual objective function depends **only on the dot product** between input pairs $(\mathbf{x}_i \cdot \mathbf{x}_j)$. We can replace $(\mathbf{x}_i \cdot \mathbf{x}_j)$ with a Kernel function $K(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j)$ to operate in infinite-dimensional feature space without ever explicitly computing high-dimensional coordinates!
